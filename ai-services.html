Three ways we work on the AI side. Most people start in one and drift into another. Each one below opens with something we hear a lot; find the one that sounds like you.

Train

Hands-on sessions with the tools you have

“How do we actually use this on our own work?”

Team workshops

Half a day or a full day with your team, working on their own files rather than made-up examples.

“How do we keep this from fading after a month?”

Ongoing training

A session a month. The tools keep changing and people keep joining; this keeps everyone level.

Strategize

Deciding what to do before anyone builds anything

Short pieces of work that end in a written plan you can act on with us or without us.

“Where would this actually save us time?”

Readiness audit

We work through your workflows, tools and data, then write up where the time goes and what’s worth changing.

Request an audit →

“Everyone has ideas. Which ones are worth doing?”

Opportunity assessment

We take the list of things you could do with AI and put numbers against them: what each one would save, what it would take to build, and which two to start with.

Scope an assessment →

“What should we roll out, and what should we hold back?”

Systems that fit what you already run

Nothing gets ripped out, and you won't end up on a platform we control.

“Could something handle this step without us?”

An agent that does one job in your workflow: triaging requests, pulling figures together, drafting the first version of a routine document. You sign off its work until you’re happy not to.
